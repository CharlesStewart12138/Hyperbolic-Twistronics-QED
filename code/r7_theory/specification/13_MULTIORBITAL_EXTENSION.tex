\section{Finite-orbital extension}
\label{sec:r7-multiorbital}

\begin{theorem}[Matrix-valued inheritance]
\label{thm:r7-matrix}
Let the fibre dimension $m<\infty$ and suppose, in operator norm on
$M_m(\mathbb C)$,
\begin{align}
 \|T_\theta(x,y)\|&\le C_0e^{-\mu d_\theta(x,y)},\\
 \|\partial_\theta T_\theta(x,y)\|&\le
 \mu R\sinh(L/R)C_0e^{-\mu d_\theta(x,y)}
 \quad (x,y\in B_L),
 \label{eq:r7-matrix-assumptions}
\end{align}
with $\mu>\kappa$, and assume the finite-range intralayer row sum is at most
$C_\parallel$.  Then all conclusions of
Theorems~\ref{thm:r7-wellposed}, \ref{thm:r7-twist},
\ref{thm:r7-ct}, and \ref{thm:r7-dyn} hold after replacing
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm Schur}\mapsto C_0S(\mu),\qquad
 M\mapsto C_\parallel+C_0S(\mu).
\end{equation}
If a matrix-valued anchor has the same five strict margins, the generic
inheritance theorem holds with the correspondingly replaced constants.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The block Schur test uses matrix norms and is independent of $m$.  The
weighted-conjugation proofs use only submultiplicativity, self-adjointness,
and the row-sum majorant.  Duhamel and resolvent identities are unchanged.
The strict-margin argument is metric-space continuity and therefore carries
over verbatim.
\end{proof}

This is a theorem about any finite fibre satisfying
\eqref{eq:r7-matrix-assumptions}.  It does not assert that the manuscript's
symbolic $s/p_1/p_2$ Slater--Koster functions have been numerically frozen or
validated.
