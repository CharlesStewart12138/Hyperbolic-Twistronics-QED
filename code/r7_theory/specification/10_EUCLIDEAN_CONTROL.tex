\section{Euclidean negative control}
\label{sec:r7-euclidean}

\begin{theorem}[Geometry and commensurability do not imply magic]
\label{thm:r7-euclidean}
For the frozen square five-state first-star Hamiltonian, the normalized
quadratic response satisfies, for every positive coupling,
\begin{equation}
 c_\square(\alpha)\ge4\sqrt2-5>0.
\end{equation}
Hence it has no positive quadratic magic root and cannot satisfy the anchor
hypotheses used in Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-inheritance}, even at exactly
commensurate twists.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is the exact algebraic minimum proved in the frozen 5203 Euclidean
control.  Positivity excludes the requisite kinetic cancellation.  Since the
inheritance theorem starts from a strict microscopic anchor rather than from
geometry alone, commensurability cannot repair the missing root.
\end{proof}

Thus negative curvature is mechanism-enabling in the declared microscopic
comparison, but curvature is not asserted to force magic for arbitrary
Hamiltonians.
