\section{A fixed generic angle and the three distinct limits}
\label{sec:r7-fixed-angle}

\begin{theorem}[Exact infinite-system finite-resolution magic]
\label{thm:r7-fixed-generic}
Fix any
$\theta\in I_{\rm magic}\setminus\Theta_C$ from
Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-inheritance}.  Then the exact, nonperiodic, bounded
self-adjoint operator $H(\theta)$ has the strict five-component certificate
\eqref{eq:r7-strict-magic} at the declared finite $(\ell,\eta,T)$.  In
particular its broadened local spectral measure
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\psi,\eta}(E)=
 -\pi^{-1}\Im\langle\psi,(E+i\eta-H(\theta))^{-1}\psi\rangle
\end{equation}
and its finite-time dynamics are infinite-system observables; they are not
properties of a Dirichlet cluster.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The finite matrices used in the proof converge to these exact observables by
Corollary~\ref{cor:r7-res-trunc} and Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-dyn}.  The perturbation
budgets in Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-inheritance} leave all inequalities strict.
R5 excludes an exact finite common cover at the selected angle.
\end{proof}

This theorem closes the fixed-generic-angle problem in the broadened local
spectral and finite-time senses requested by R7.  It does not merge the
following limits:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\ell\to\infty$ requires a simultaneously shrinking angular window as
in \eqref{eq:r7-exponential-window};
\item $\eta\downarrow0$ requires local spectral regularity (for a density) or
control of atoms/singular continuous measure;
\item $T\to\infty$ requires spectral-type or transport information, such as a
mobility gap, dynamical localization, a Mourre estimate, or an appropriate
RAGE decomposition.
\end{enumerate}
Finite moments and a Hodge Hessian do not determine spectral type, so none of
these extra hypotheses follows from the frozen inputs.  This is the precise
obstruction to the stronger zero-resolution/infinite-time slogan.  It does
not weaken Theorem~\ref{thm:r7-fixed-generic}.
